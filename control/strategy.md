# Strategy

*Layer 3 — human-merged at birth, earns its way down the authorization ladder
like any contract. This is Meristem's own standing hypothesis about what is
worth working on now. It is meant to be rewritten as evidence arrives.*

## Current hypothesis (v1, authored by the owner at birth)

P0's only real question is whether the loop closes at all. Everything else is
premature.

1. **Prove the cycle runs.** Task in, mutation out, gates fire, candidate
   promoted or rejected, journal tells the truth about which.
2. **Prove the gates bite.** Golden fixtures must fail every cycle. An immune
   system nobody has ever seen reject anything is decoration.
3. **Prove the seed can change itself.** At least three accepted mutations to
   `meristem/` — through gates, canary, promotion, restart.
4. **Prove the germline executes.** One trivial organ through the full
   lifecycle, then pruned. A protocol that has never run is spec fiction.
5. **Measure Tier-A reliability.** Whole-file replacement at this scale is an
   assumption, not a fact. If it holds, the cheap path stays default; if it
   fails often, Tier B is the answer and the cost argument still holds.

## Deliberately not yet

- `reflect` — the human is the first reflect. P1 hands over the *capability
  gap*, not a parts list, and sees what decomposition the seed proposes.
- Organs of substance — body stays empty until real work demands something.
- Succession — designed, stubbed, exercised at P3.
- External benchmark anchors — P3. Until then, anchors are thin and the
  divergence alarm is the main defence against self-deception.

## Open questions this strategy cannot yet answer

- Is a heterogeneous cheap review panel actually better than one strong
  reviewer? The fixture catch rate is the instrument.
- Does self-authored probe design produce discriminating probes, or
  comfortable ones?
- Where does the first real capability gap appear — and will the seed notice
  it before the human does?
